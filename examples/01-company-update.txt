Northwind Robotics raises prices as warehouse orders double

Northwind Robotics said on Tuesday that orders for its warehouse picking arms doubled in the three months to March, and that it will raise prices by 8% from June to cover the cost of a second factory.

The company, which is based in Leeds, sold 1,240 arms in the quarter, up from 610 a year earlier. Revenue rose to £38.2 million. Its chief executive, Priya Raman, said the jump came mostly from grocery chains that had trialled the arms last autumn and then ordered them for every depot.

"They ran one arm in one aisle for six weeks," she said. "When the error rate came in under the human baseline, the conversation changed from whether to how fast."

Raman said the new factory in Wakefield would open next spring and roughly triple output. The firm has borrowed £60 million for it, and the price rise is meant to repay that debt within four years rather than seven.

Not every customer is pleased. Halden Foods, one of its first buyers, said it would pause further orders until it had seen the new price list. Its operations director, Tom Alvarez, said the arms had paid for themselves in under two years, but that an 8% rise "changes the arithmetic for the smaller sites".

Northwind expects to ship its first arms from the new site in the second quarter of next year. Analysts at Pennine Securities raised their forecast for the year to £150 million in revenue, up from £120 million in January.
