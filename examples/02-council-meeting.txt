Ashby council approves river crossing after two-year delay

Ashby District Council voted on Wednesday night to build a pedestrian and cycle bridge over the River Mease, two years after the scheme was first put to councillors and later withdrawn over its cost.

The bridge will link the Moira Road estate with the town centre, cutting a 2.4-mile walk to under half a mile. It is expected to cost £6.8 million, of which £5 million comes from a government active-travel grant awarded last April. The council will cover the rest from its capital budget.

Councillor Helen Okafor, who leads on transport, said the grant had been decisive. "Two years ago we were being asked to find all of it ourselves," she told the meeting. "Now we are finding a quarter of it, and the case makes itself."

The vote passed 31 to 9. Most of those against were members of the opposition group, whose leader, Martin Shaw, argued that the money would be better spent on repairing existing roads. He said the council had a repair backlog of £14 million and that the bridge "solves a problem for one estate while every estate has potholes".

Okafor said the two budgets could not be swapped: the grant can only be spent on walking and cycling schemes, and would be returned to the government if the bridge were not built by the end of next year.

Construction is due to begin in September and take fourteen months. The council will hold a public exhibition of the final design next month at Ashby library.
